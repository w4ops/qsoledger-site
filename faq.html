About QSO Ledger

What is QSO Ledger?

It's a logbook app built just for the Mac. Your recent contacts show at the top of the window, and the boxes where you type a new contact sit below them. Type the other station's call sign, press Tab to move from box to box, then press Enter to save. That's it.

QSO Ledger can also:

  • Control your radio over CAT
  • Show DX cluster and POTA spots
  • Upload your contacts to LoTW, eQSL, QRZ Logbook, Club Log and World Radio League
  • Track WAS, DXCC, grid and county progress
Who makes it?

QSO Ledger is made by W4OPS. It's an independent app and isn't run by any club or organization.

Is QSO Ledger connected to N3FJP Software?

No, but N3FJP played a part in how it came about. AC Log is one of the most popular loggers in ham radio, and many hams who moved to a Mac missed its simple layout. So before building QSO Ledger, we reached out to Scott, N3FJP, to ask his permission to model our interface after his software, and he gave us his blessing. We then put our own spin on it, and wrote QSO Ledger from scratch as a native Mac app. N3FJP and AC Log are trademarks of Scott, N3FJP. QSO Ledger is not affiliated with or endorsed by N3FJP Software.

On Windows?

To learn more about N3FJP's software, visit www.n3fjp.com. Whether you're logging everyday QSOs, working contests or taking part in Field Day, we can't recommend it enough.

I use AC Log on Windows. Why would I switch?

If AC Log on a Windows PC works for you, there's no need to switch. QSO Ledger is for hams who want that same easy style of logging on a Mac, with no Windows virtual machine, no Wine, and no USB pass-through problems for the radio.

Does it run on Windows, Linux or iPhone?

Right now it runs only on the Mac. An iPhone version is planned. There are no plans for Windows or Linux. If you're on Windows, we strongly recommend N3FJP's software, available at www.n3fjp.com. It's an excellent choice for everyday QSOs, contests and Field Day.

Which Macs can run it?

Any Mac with macOS 14 Sonoma or newer. It runs natively on both Apple Silicon Macs (M1 and newer) and older Intel Macs.

Do I need the internet?

No. Your log is saved on your Mac, so logging, searching, awards, printing and ADIF import and export all work offline. Features that need the internet, like spots, call sign lookups and uploads, pick back up when you reconnect.

Price, free trial and buying

How much does it cost?

QSO Ledger is free to download from the Mac App Store, and every feature works for the first 30 days. After that, a one-time $39.99 purchase inside the app unlocks it for good. There is no monthly subscription.

Are updates free?

Yes. Your one-time unlock includes all future updates to QSO Ledger for Mac.

What happens when the 30-day trial ends if I haven't bought it?

Nothing is lost. You can still open, search, print and export your log as an ADIF file. You can also keep logging for free: 15 new contacts a day (UTC) for the first three months after the trial, then 10 a day. Contacts from WSJT-X, other apps and the API count toward the same daily allowance. Unlocking removes the limit.

I bought it on one Mac. Do I pay again for my other Mac?

No. On the other Mac, sign in to the Mac App Store with the same Apple ID. Then choose QSO Ledger → Unlock QSO Ledger… → Restore Purchase.

Can my family use my purchase?

The unlock can't be shared through Apple's Family Sharing, so each Apple ID needs its own. But if your family shares one Mac, everyone can use that one unlocked copy, and each ham can keep a separate log. See "Several hams share one Mac" below.

Can our club buy one copy for Field Day?

Each Mac unlocks through its own Apple ID, so every Mac needs its own unlock or can use its 30-day trial. Several Macs logging into one shared Field Day log isn't available yet.

What is the refund policy?

Apple handles all purchases and refunds. You can ask for a refund at reportaproblem.apple.com.

How do I try the beta?

First install Apple's free TestFlight app. Then join the public beta at testflight.apple.com/join/hj4aNTzw.

Getting started

How do I set it up the first time?

When you first open QSO Ledger, a Welcome window asks for your call sign, name, grid square and location. It also asks you to pick a folder for backups. You can change any of this later in Settings → Station and Settings → Backup. To see the Welcome window again, choose Help → Welcome….

Is there a Save button?

No, you don't need one. Each contact is saved the moment you log it.

I closed the main window. How do I get it back?

Click the QSO Ledger icon in the Dock, or choose Window → QSO Ledger (⌘0). Closing the window doesn't lose anything.

How do I clear the boxes without logging the contact?

Press Esc.

Are the times in UTC?

Yes. Dates and times are logged in UTC. In Settings → Station you can choose how dates look when you see and type them. Behind the scenes, contacts are always saved in standard ADIF format.

How do I type in an old paper log?

Type each contact's date and time into the boxes yourself. To save typing, go to Settings → Station and set the date option to Last entry date. Each new contact will then start with the date of the one before it.

Where's the full manual?

Choose Help → QSO Ledger Help (⌘?). It covers common questions and every menu, and you can search it.

Bringing your old log over

Can I bring in my log from N3FJP AC Log?

Yes. In AC Log, export your log as an ADIF file. Then in QSO Ledger, choose File → Import ADIF… (⇧⌘I) and pick that file. Your AC Log file stays exactly as it was.

What about other loggers, like RUMlogNG, MacLoggerDX, HAMRS, Log4OM or LoTW?

It works the same way. Almost every logging program can export an ADIF file (it ends in .adi or .adif). Export one, then use File → Import ADIF….

What gets brought over?

Call signs, dates, times, bands, frequencies, modes, signal reports, names, locations, grid squares, counties, POTA park numbers, comments, and your QSL, LoTW and eQSL status. QSO Ledger then works out your WAS, DXCC and grid totals for you.

If I import the same file twice, will I get duplicates?

No. Contacts already in your log are skipped. When it's done, QSO Ledger tells you how many contacts were added, how many were already there, and how many couldn't be read.

My ADIF file lists the same contact twice. What happens?

The two copies are combined into one, and the confirmed copy is kept. When the import finishes, click Review Merged Contacts… to see what was combined.

My log is in a spreadsheet. Can I import it?

Not directly. First convert the spreadsheet to an ADIF file. Several free online tools and ham programs can do this. Then use File → Import ADIF….

How big a log can it handle?

QSO Ledger has been tested with a log of 100,000 contacts.

Logging contacts

What happens when I type a call sign?

When you press Tab to leave the Call box, QSO Ledger fills in the station's country, continent, CQ and ITU zones, and the beam heading and distance. If you've set up a callbook, it can also fill in their name, location and grid square. You'll also see how many times you've worked that station and when you last did.

What does "DUPE!" mean?

It means you've already worked that call sign on the same band and mode. It's only a heads-up. You can still log the contact.

What do the red New! tags mean?

They point out a first: a new call sign, a new country, or a new band or mode for a country or call. You can choose which alerts you see in Settings → Station.

It says "No country found. Log anyway?"

The call sign doesn't match any country prefix QSO Ledger knows. First, check for a typo. Special-event calls and brand-new calls sometimes cause this too. If the call is correct, choose Log Anyway.

Am I stuck with the modes in the list?

No. QSO Ledger knows all the standard ADIF modes, including FT8, FT4, JS8, PSK31 and RTTY, so LoTW and other services will accept your contacts.

Can I choose which boxes show, and in what order?

Yes. In Settings → Fields & Columns, pick the boxes for the top row and bottom row, and drag them into the order you like. Pressing Tab follows that order. The Call box is always first.

Can I change the columns in Recent Contacts?

Yes. Pick the columns and their order in Settings → Fields & Columns, or right-click any column title. Drag the edge of a column to make it wider or narrower. Reset Columns puts everything back the way it started.

Can I see my earlier contacts with a station while I'm logging?

Yes. Turn on the switch under Recent Contacts in Settings → Fields & Columns. When you Tab out of the Call box, the list shows your past contacts with that station.

How do I fix a contact?

Right-click it in Recent Contacts and choose Edit.

How do I change many contacts at once?

Select them by holding ⌘ or Shift while you click. Then right-click and choose Edit Contacts…. Only the boxes you type in will change, and QSO Ledger asks before saving anything.

I logged a lot of contacts offline. Can it fill in the missing details?

Yes. Choose Edit → Fill Blank Fields from Call…. It fills in empty boxes, like country, zones, band and callbook details, for your whole log, the contacts you've selected, or a range of record numbers. It never changes a box that already has something in it.

How do I find a contact?

Type a call, name, state, country, county or comment in the search box at the top left of Recent Contacts and press Return. ⌘F jumps to the box. Clear it to go back to the list you had before.

Why are western longitudes shown as negative numbers?

QSO Ledger uses the standard map rule: north and east are positive, south and west are negative. If latitude and longitude are blank, direction and distance are figured from the center of the station's grid square.

More than one log, more than one ham

Can I keep more than one log?

Yes. QSO Ledger calls each log a ledger. Add ledgers in Settings → Ledgers, for example one for nets, one for Field Day, and one for each POTA activation. Each ledger has its own station details.

What's the General Ledger?

It's your main log. Each ledger can send ("post") its contacts to the General Ledger, and only those ledgers count toward awards. To see every posting ledger together, choose Ledger → Open General Ledger (⇧⌘G).

Where did my contacts go?

Check which ledger is picked in the Ledger menu. Each ledger shows only its own contacts. Open the General Ledger to see them all together.

Several hams share one Mac. Can we each have our own log?

Yes. Choose File → New Repository… to make a separate space for each ham, with its own ledgers, station details, online logbook logins, Watch List and backups. Use File → Switch Repository to move between them. To keep your repository private, give it a password in Settings → Station. Hold the Option key while QSO Ledger opens to choose which repository to open.

I got a new call sign. What do I do?

Change it in Settings → Station. Contacts you already logged keep the call you used at the time. If you want the new call's contacts kept separate, start a new ledger.

Backups and moving to a new Mac

Where is my log stored?

On your Mac. If you use Time Machine, it backs up your log along with everything else.

Does QSO Ledger make its own backups?

Yes. In Settings → Backup, pick a folder, such as an external drive or a cloud folder like iCloud Drive or Dropbox. QSO Ledger saves a standard ADIF backup there every day, every week, or each time you quit. It keeps the newest 3 to 10 backups (7 unless you change it).

How do I restore a backup?

Choose File → Restore from Backup…. It only adds contacts that are missing, puts each one back in its own ledger, and never deletes anything.

How do I move QSO Ledger to a new Mac?

Install QSO Ledger on the new Mac. Then choose File → Restore from Backup… and pick a backup file. Or, on the old Mac, choose File → Export Repository… and restore that file on the new one. Use Restore Purchase to bring back your unlock.

What's the best way to never lose my log?

Use more than one kind of backup:

  1. Keep Time Machine turned on.
  2. Point Settings → Backup at a second drive or a cloud folder.
  3. Every so often, choose File → Export ADIF… (⇧⌘E) and keep that file somewhere else too.
Does iCloud keep my log in sync between Macs?

Not yet, but it's planned. For now, use backups or ADIF export to move contacts between Macs.

Can I export just part of my log?

Yes. File → Export ADIF… can save the whole log, only the contacts you've selected, or a range of record numbers.

Radio control (CAT)

Which radios can QSO Ledger control?

Icom, Kenwood, Elecraft, Yaesu and FlexRadio radios work directly. Hundreds more work through helper programs like Hamlib's rigctld, flrig, or TCI (used by ExpertSDR, Thetis and other SDR programs).

What does radio control do for me?

As you tune your radio, the frequency, band and mode in QSO Ledger change to match. And when you click a DX or POTA spot, your radio jumps to that station.

How do I connect my radio?

Go to Settings → Radio. Choose your radio type and the USB port it's plugged into. If your radio is on the network instead, choose rigctld, flrig, TCI, or TCP (for programs like SmartSDR CAT, Thetis or ser2net).

What address does my Icom need?

Use the radio's CI-V address. Here are the common ones:

RadioCI-V address
IC-730094
IC-705A4
IC-9700A2
IC-761098
IC-710088
IC-78518E

The address and baud rate must be the same in the radio's menu and in QSO Ledger.

My radio's USB port isn't in the list.

Try these steps:

  1. Install the radio maker's USB driver if it needs one. Many Silicon Labs and FTDI cables do.
  2. Unplug the cable and plug it back in.
  3. Close and reopen Settings → Radio.

On a Mac, radio ports have names like cu.usbserial-XXXX or cu.SLAB_USBtoUART.

Can two programs control the radio at the same time?

Only one program at a time can use a USB port. To share your radio between QSO Ledger and a program like WSJT-X or fldigi, run flrig or Hamlib's rigctld, then point both programs at it.

Can I use Hamlib for my radio and my rotator at the same time?

Yes. rigctld (port 4532) runs the radio and rotctld (port 4533) runs the rotator. Each one needs its own USB port.

Does it work with a remote station?

Yes, as long as your radio or its control program shares CAT over the network through rigctld, flrig, TCI or a TCP port. Your remote-station software handles the audio.

Digital modes and other programs

Can WSJT-X and JTDX log straight into QSO Ledger?

Yes. Here's how:

  1. In QSO Ledger, turn on Settings → Radio → Log contacts from WSJT-X and JTDX.
  2. In WSJT-X or JTDX, open Settings → Reporting.
  3. Set the UDP Server to 127.0.0.1 and the port to 2237.

Now every contact you log in WSJT-X or JTDX goes right into your current ledger.

Does it work with JS8Call, fldigi, GridTracker and Hunterlog?

Yes. QSO Ledger includes an N3FJP-compatible API, so any program that can log to AC Log can log to QSO Ledger without changes. To set it up:

  1. Turn it on in Settings → Radio. It uses TCP port 1100 unless you change it.
  2. In the other program, choose its N3FJP logging option and point it at this Mac.
Can programs on another computer log to QSO Ledger?

Yes, if you allow network access for the API in Settings → Radio. Only do this on a network you trust.

Does QSO Ledger have its own API for scripts, Stream Deck and Apple Shortcuts? Next build

Yes. The QSO Ledger API lets other programs on your Mac talk to QSO Ledger at http://127.0.0.1:7373/v1/. Programs can:

  • Log contacts, search the log and check for dupes
  • Look up a call sign and fill in the entry form
  • Export ADIF and switch ledgers
  • Read and tune the radio, and send CW
  • Get live updates as things happen

Turn it on in Settings → Radio → QSO Ledger API. That screen also shows the access key that every request needs. The API is off until you turn it on, and it can never delete contacts. After the free trial ends, programs can still read your log, and contacts they add count toward the free daily allowance until you unlock. A page listing every command, with examples, comes with the next build.

What's the difference between the two APIs?

The N3FJP-compatible API is for programs that already work with N3FJP. The QSO Ledger API is for anything new, and it can do much more. You can run both at the same time.

Will you add support for my favorite program?

Email support@qsoledger.com with the program's name and what you'd like it to do.

DX spots and POTA

How do I see DX spots?

In Settings → DX Cluster, check one or more spot sources: NC7J, VE7CC, W3LPL, K1TTT, WA9PIE, DXSpots.com or POTA Spots. They all connect at once, and if several report the same spot, you'll see it only once. To use a cluster that isn't listed, add it under Custom cluster.

How do I filter spots?

Use Filters in Settings → DX Cluster:

  • Heard by is about the spotter: show spots only from stations on certain continents, or only in your country.
  • Station heard is about the DX station itself.
  • You can also hide whole bands, or hide CW, digital or phone spots.

Show All Spots clears every filter.

My usual cluster is down.

Check a different one in Settings → DX Cluster. Auto reconnect is on unless you turn it off. It keeps retrying a cluster that dropped, and also reconnects after your Mac sleeps, after a network drop, or after 10 quiet minutes.

Can I send login commands, like SET/NOANN or SHOW/DX?

Yes. Add them under login commands in Settings → DX Cluster.

What happens when I click a spot?

The call sign goes into the entry form, and your radio tunes to the spot's frequency and mode. If you have a rotator connected, your beam can turn toward the station too.

Can I hide the spots or move them?

To hide them, uncheck Settings → Show DX Spots. To move them, use Settings → Window Layout: Traditional puts spots under the entry boxes, and Side Panel puts them on the right. Drag the bar above the spots to make the area bigger or smaller.

Can the spots have their own window? Next build

Yes. Click the pop-out button on the DX Spots title, or use the Settings menu. You can move that window to another screen. Close it and the spots go back into the main window.

How do I get an alert when a station I'm hunting shows up?

Add the call to File → Watch List…. You'll get a pop-up when you type that call, and its spots are marked with a star.

Does QSO Ledger support Parks on the Air (POTA)?

Yes!

  • Hunting parks: Check POTA Spots in Settings → DX Cluster to see who is activating a park right now.
  • Logging park numbers: Add the Park Ref box in Settings → Fields & Columns.
  • Activating a park yourself: Make a separate ledger for each activation. When you're done, export it as ADIF and upload it to pota.app. Set that ledger to post to the General Ledger so your contacts count toward awards.

Online logbooks and QSL cards

Which online logbooks does QSO Ledger work with?

ARRL Logbook of the World (LoTW), eQSL, QRZ Logbook, Club Log and World Radio League. Enter your logins in Settings → eLogs. Your passwords are kept safely in your Mac's Keychain.

How do I upload to LoTW?
  1. Install ARRL's free TQSL program and set up your call sign certificate.
  2. In QSO Ledger, choose eLogs → Logbook of the World → Upload New Contacts (TQSL)….

TQSL signs the upload with your certificate and sends it to LoTW.

How do I get my LoTW confirmations?

Choose eLogs → Logbook of the World → Download Confirmations. It uses your LoTW website login and marks which contacts are confirmed.

Does it upload only my new contacts?

Yes. Upload New Contacts sends only the contacts that haven't gone to that service yet.

What do I need for QRZ Logbook?

Your QRZ Logbook API key. Find it on qrz.com under My Logbook → Settings. Uploading needs a QRZ Logbook subscription.

How do I connect World Radio League?
  1. In World Radio League, open Integrations → Developer API and choose Generate API key.
  2. Paste the key into Settings → eLogs right away. WRL only shows it once.
  3. Choose eLogs → World Radio League → Upload New Contacts or Download New Contacts.
What do the row colors in Recent Contacts mean?

They show the QSL status of each contact: not sent, sent, received, or both sent and received. You can change the colors in Settings → Colors & Fonts.

How do I mark paper QSL cards?

Select the contacts, right-click, and choose Mark QSL Sent or Mark QSL Rcvd.

Can I print QSL mailing labels?

Yes. Choose File → Print QSL Labels…. It prints on Avery 5160, 5161, 5163 or 5164 label sheets. You can start partway down a sheet you've already used, and mark the contacts as QSL Sent when you're done. Addresses come from your callbook.

Callbook lookups

Which callbooks does it use?

QRZ.com (this needs a QRZ XML subscription) or HamQTH (free account). Set up either one in Settings → eLogs.

Do I need a callbook?

No. QSO Ledger works fine without one. It still fills in the country, zones, direction and distance using its built-in country file.

How do I look up a call sign?
  • CallBook → Look Up Call (⌘L) fills in any empty boxes.
  • CallBook → Show Callbook Card (⌘K) shows the station's photo, address and QSL details.
  • Open Call on QRZ.com (⇧⌘L) opens the station's page on QRZ.com.
My QRZ lookup only shows a name.

That usually means your QRZ account doesn't have an XML subscription. Full lookups need one.

How do I keep the country list up to date?

Use Settings → Country File to update the file (cty.dat) that QSO Ledger uses for countries and zones. Update it whenever new DXCC entities or call sign prefixes are announced.

Awards and statistics

Which awards does QSO Ledger track?

It tracks your worked and confirmed totals for Worked All States, DXCC, counties, grid squares and more, with maps. Open them with Awards → Worked All States and DXCC… (⇧⌘A).

Which contacts count toward awards?

Only contacts in ledgers that post to the General Ledger. Turn Post to General Ledger on or off for each ledger in Settings → Ledgers.

How do the maps work?

Places you've worked and confirmed are shaded in. Drag to move around the map, and pinch to zoom in or out.

Can I see statistics about my log?

Yes. Awards → Log Statistics… (⌥⌘S) shows your total contacts, unique call signs, countries, states, grid squares and your busiest day. It also breaks down contacts by band and mode, by year, and by your most-worked countries. You can filter by ledger, year, band or mode, then copy or print the results.

Contests and Field Day

Can I use QSO Ledger for contests?

For casual contesting, yes. Keep a separate ledger for the contest. When it's over, use File → Export Cabrillo… to save your log with your categories, the exchange you sent, and serial numbers. Live contest scoring isn't available yet.

How do I send in a contest log?

Choose File → Export Cabrillo…. Pick the contest, your categories and exchange, choose the contacts, and save the .log file. Then upload or email it to the contest's log robot.

Can I use it for Field Day?

Yes, on one Mac. Make a Field Day ledger, and when you're done, export it as ARRL-FD Cabrillo. A special Field Day screen and several Macs sharing one log aren't available yet.

We used several Macs with no network. Can we combine the logs?

Yes. Export each Mac's Field Day ledger as ADIF, then import them all into one ledger on one Mac. Contacts that are already in the log are skipped, so you won't get duplicates.

Sending CW Next build

Can QSO Ledger send CW?

Yes. It can use your radio's built-in keyer or a K1EL WinKeyer. Choose which one in Settings → CW.

Which radios can send CW through CAT?

Icom, Kenwood, Elecraft and FlexRadio, plus any radio controlled through Hamlib, flrig or TCI. Yaesu radios don't have a CAT command for sending typed text, so with a Yaesu, use flrig or a WinKeyer.

How do the F-key messages work?

Keys F1 through F8 each send a message you write in Settings → CW. Messages can include fill-in tags that QSO Ledger swaps for real information when it sends:

TagSends
{CALL}The other station's call sign
{RST}The signal report
{NAME}The other operator's name
{STATE}The other station's state
{MYCALL}Your call sign
{MYNAME}Your name
{MYGRID}Your grid square

For example, {CALL} DE {MYCALL} UR {RST} NAME {MYNAME} might send "K4ABC DE W4OPS UR 599 NAME MIKE".

How do I stop sending, or change the speed?

Press Esc to stop sending right away. The CW bar under the entry boxes has a Stop button, the speed, the F1–F8 buttons, and a box for typing any text. The CW menu also has Faster and Slower.

Rotator control Next build

Can QSO Ledger turn my beam?

Yes. In Settings → Rotator, choose your rotator controller and its USB port. These work directly:

  • Yaesu GS-232A or GS-232B (Green Heron, ERC and K3NG controllers use the same commands)
  • SPID / AlphaSpid Rot2Prog
  • Hy-Gain DCU-1 or Rotor-EZ
  • EasyComm II
  • Prosistel

For any other rotator, use Hamlib's rotctld (port 4533).

Do I need to install Hamlib?

Not for the controllers listed above, because QSO Ledger talks to them directly. You only need Hamlib for other rotators.

How do I turn the beam?
  • Rotator → Turn to Short Path (⌘R) aims at the station in the entry form.
  • Rotator → Turn to Long Path (⇧⌘R) aims the long path.
  • Rotator → Stop (⌘.) stops the rotator.

QSO Ledger aims using the station's grid square, or its country if there's no grid. You can also type any heading on the Rotator card, or right-click a spot and choose Turn Beam to ….

Can the beam turn by itself?

Yes. Under When to turn in Settings → Rotator, choose whether it turns when you leave the Call box or when you click a spot. You can also list country prefixes (for example VK, ZL) that you usually work by long path.

My rotator stops at south, or has 450° rotation.

In Settings → Rotator, set Rotator stops at (North or South) and the over-travel option. That way QSO Ledger knows which way the rotator has to go around. If your antenna isn't lined up exactly with the rotator's north, use Beam offset to correct it.

Where's the Rotator card?

It's in the Side Panel layout. It can also open in its own window from either layout.

Nets, printing and the look of the app

Does QSO Ledger help me run a net?

Yes. Net → Net Manager… (⇧⌘N) keeps a check-in list. Each row saves as you type, so nothing is lost even if your Mac crashes. When the net ends, Log and Close asks which ledger the check-ins go into. Save As… makes a PDF or spreadsheet, and Share Roster sends the PDF.

How do I print my log?

Choose File → Print Logbook… (⌘P). It prints the call, date, time, band, frequency, mode, reports and country, oldest first, with column headings on every page. To make a PDF instead, choose Save as PDF in the Print window.

Can I print a list for an award application?

Filter to the contacts you need, then print them or export them as ADIF. Awards → Log Statistics… can print your totals too.

Does it have Dark mode?

Yes. In Settings → Colors & Fonts, choose Light, Dark, or Match macOS.

Can I change the colors and fonts?

Yes. Pick a ready-made theme, or set your own colors and text sizes for the boxes, labels and contact list in Settings → Colors & Fonts.

Can I rearrange the window?

Yes. From the Settings menu, choose Traditional Layout (spots under the entry boxes) or Side Panel Layout (spots and station details on the right). You can also reorder the entry boxes and columns in Settings → Fields & Columns.

Privacy

Does QSO Ledger collect my data?

Your log stays on your Mac. QSO Ledger sends only anonymous counts, like how many contacts were logged and on which bands and modes. It never sends call signs, names, grid squares, frequencies, park numbers or notes. To turn this off, switch off Share anonymous usage statistics in Settings. See the Privacy page for details.

Where are my passwords kept?

In your Mac's Keychain, the same secure place macOS keeps your other passwords.

Do I need to create an account?

No. There's no QSO Ledger account to sign up for.

Help and support

Something that used to work suddenly stopped.

Check your login in Settings → eLogs, or your radio connection in Settings → Radio. Online services sometimes change how they work, so check that service's website too. Still stuck? Email us.

My radio won't connect.

Make sure the radio type, port and baud rate in QSO Ledger match the settings in your radio's own menu, and that no other program is using the port. For an Icom, check the CI-V address too.

How do I report a bug or ask for a new feature?

Email support@qsoledger.com. Please include your macOS version, your QSO Ledger version, and what you were doing when the problem happened. A screenshot helps a lot.

Is there a user group?

Yes. Join the QSO Ledger Facebook group at facebook.com/groups/qsoledger.
