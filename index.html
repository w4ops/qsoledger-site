Questions

Ham radio logging on a Mac, answered

Is QSO Ledger a copy of N3FJP?

No. QSO Ledger is heavily inspired by N3FJP's AC Log, but it isn't a direct copy. It may not have every feature AC Log has, and it has some that AC Log doesn't. Everything has been designed and optimized for the Mac.

Is there an N3FJP logger for Mac?

N3FJP Software makes its loggers for Windows. QSO Ledger is a separate, native Mac ham radio logbook with a similar simple layout. It is not affiliated with N3FJP Software.

Can I move my N3FJP AC Log contacts to my Mac?

Yes. In AC Log, export an ADIF file. In QSO Ledger, choose File → Import ADIF. Your contacts, QSL status and award progress come with them.

Does QSO Ledger work offline?

Yes. Your logbook lives on your Mac, so you can log in the shack, at a park or on Field Day with no internet.

Does it support POTA, CAT control and DX clusters?

Yes. It shows live POTA spots and several DX clusters at once, and reads frequency and mode from Icom, Yaesu, Kenwood and Elecraft radios or rigctld.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac with macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple Silicon or Intel.
